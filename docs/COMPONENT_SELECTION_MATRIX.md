# IDEAL Component Selection Matrix

**Status:** Draft research matrix — no runtime dependencies selected or adopted.  
**Last reviewed:** 16 November 2026  
**Phase:** 01 — Open-source and infrastructure research  
**Scope:** Initial crypto perpetual-futures system. Spot accumulation and equities remain deferred.

This matrix consolidates the repository landscape review, proof-of-concept evidence, historical-data tests, licensing and deployment constraints. It is a decision aid, not an architecture approval. Adoption requires a pinned version/commit, source and test review, licence/dependency review, bounded integration plan, resource benchmark and explicit owner approval.

## Decision labels

- **Preferred POC candidate:** strongest current candidate for further isolated testing; not production-approved.
- **Research/reference only:** useful ideas or code patterns, but not selected as a dependency.
- **Hold:** potentially useful, but a material legal, data-quality, maintenance or cost gate is unresolved.
- **Rejected for current scope:** does not meet the initial perpetual-futures requirement or has an unacceptable constraint.

## Cross-cutting hard gates

1. **New Zealand derivatives eligibility:** FMA guidance says recurrently traded products commonly called futures/swaps/CFDs/margin contracts can be derivatives regardless of duration, and a derivatives issuer making a regulated offer to NZ retail investors must hold the relevant licence. FMC Act Part 3 has NZ territorial scope for offers received in NZ, subject to statutory exceptions. This is not a finding that a user merely operating personal trading software must be licensed. Exact offshore contracting entity, product terms, customer classification and cross-border treatment require independent legal advice. **Phase 01 disposition: all venue selection and exchange connectivity explicitly excluded pending product/entity-specific legal review; no venue, account or API key selected.**
2. **Live data entitlement:** OKX 400-level 10 ms books-l2-tbt requires VIP4+; standard books is 100 ms. Separate books-rpi is 400 levels/100 ms; rpiTakerAccess defaults false and instrument permission must be checked. Historical access does not imply live entitlement.
3. **Sequence continuity:** OKX historical archive schema lacks sequence IDs/checksums; live books/book-rpi continuity uses seqId/prevSeqId because checksum is deprecated. Tardis normalized CSV omits exchange sequence IDs. Reconstructed valid books do not prove no lost updates.
4. **Passive fills:** L2 market-by-price is not L3; no true FIFO queue position. Do not claim passive-fill accuracy.
5. **Execution-quality evidence:** the original 7–13 Nov strict-preceding screen passed 5/7 days. Revalidation #43803546192 confirmed 7 Nov's only unmatched trade is 2 ms before the first book snapshot (497,187/497,188 align); p95 age is 32 ms. 8 Nov has zero missing/invalid book matches but 96.453% side/BBO consistency, concentrated in <=10 ms observations (96.23% vs >=99.79% in older buckets). Across 19,918,682 aligned trades, 97.726% are <=20 ms old, 99.525% <=50 ms, 99.924% <=100 ms, 99.995% <=250 ms; max daily age is 475–519 ms. Under the owner-approved policy, 7 Nov and 8 Nov are **conditional historical market-structure research days**; 9–13 Nov are preferred-quality. Original strict result remains 5/7, not retroactively passed. Preserve per-day denominators, age flags and the unmatched boundary row. No exact event ordering, queue position or passive-fill accuracy is established. Full audit: [checkpoint 034](CHATGPT_CONVERSATIONS/2026-11-16-034-okx-seven-day-boundary-and-risk-classification.md).
6. **Independent source comparison:** Tardis Binance and OKX normalized samples reconstructed. Three OKX dates (1 Sep, 1 Oct, 1 Nov) show >99.3% strict-preceding exact BBO match and 100% inclusive same-timestamp match. September/October workflow #43804760777 passed; Tardis has ~6.86x and 8.44x fewer messages than official archives, 49–65 vs 96 snapshots, and max exchange-time gaps of 2.4–16.5s. Normalized CSV omits raw channel ID; provider documentation indicates books-l2-tbt coverage through 21 May 2026 and books thereafter, but exact per-message channel provenance is not exposed. **Disposition: accept as secondary sampled BBO/reference for internal research only; not a 10ms execution-calibration source.** Tardis Terms of Service restrict use in products/services and redistribution/resale of raw or sub-10-minute data; no external distribution or product embedding without a separate written licence. Full audits checkpoints 036 and 038.
7. **$0 runtime feasibility:** Oracle A1 has the most free VM capacity (2 OCPU/12GB) but home-region capacity, seven-day idle reclamation and no SLA. Google e2-micro is US-only/0.25 vCPU/1GB/1GB egress; Render Free spins down after 15 minutes without inbound traffic and has no persistent disk. Cloudflare Workers/SQLite Durable Objects can support edge relays but outgoing WebSocket clients cannot hibernate and quotas are not a full Python/Rust engine host. GitHub Actions is CI only. Self-hosting existing hardware is the only plausible zero-subscription core path. An existing Windows PC/laptop is available as a fallback, but mobile-data caps/tethering, stable uptime, power and remote-access safety are unverified; owner prefers phone-first operation. No reliable $0 persistent production worker is proven; no provisioning or paid infrastructure. See checkpoint 035.
8. **Licensing and version pins:** NautilusTrader is LGPL-3.0-only; pytest 9.2.0 is MIT; orjson 3.11.6 is MPL-2.0 AND (Apache-2.0 OR MIT), selected because 3.11.3 is affected by CVE-2025-67221. Scoped transitive locks, SBOM and pip-audit are now complete for the parser and POC environments; repository-wide/future-product coverage remains limited. See checkpoint 041.
9. **Engine maturity:** NautilusTrader 2.0.0rc9 (10 Nov 2026) remains a pre-release; stable PyPI remains 1.231.0 with a different API. Upstream explicitly advises against 2.x release candidates for production real-capital trading. IDEAL's 84-test x86_64/ARM64 POC is strong research evidence only; liquidation, partial fills, queue behaviour, funding recovery and free-worker performance remain open.
